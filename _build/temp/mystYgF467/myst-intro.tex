\section{Introduction}

\subsection{What is \textit{random}?}

\begin{itemize}
\item Some behavior that we cannot perfectly predict, as opposed to being \textit{deterministic}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why random?}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theologian:} We can't know God's mind!
\item \textbf{Copenhagen quantum physicist:} The laws of quantum physics are fundamentally random.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What to do with random?}

\begin{itemize}
\item Although we cannot perfectly determine random behavior, we still want to obtain its specifications to some degree.
\item \textbf{Probability Theory} is the theory that systematically studies and characterizes randomness.
\item In this course, we will take an engineering approach to Probability Theory---more operational and less concerned with mathematical rigor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How to study probability?}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intuition}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{\textbf{Example:}} Dr. Wong is a nice guy; he will probably give me a good grade in EEE 5544.
\item This approach is not systematic, relies on guessing, and cannot
quantify the \textit{``likelihood''} of the behavior.
\end{itemize}


\item \textbf{Relative frequency (a.k.a. counting)}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{\textbf{Example:}} Dr. Wong taught EEE 5544 three times in the past. Altogether, 200 students took Dr. Wong's classes, and 50 of them received an $A$. Thus, the chance of receiving an $A$ in Dr. Wong's class is
\begin{equation}
\frac{50}{200}=\frac{1}{4}.
\end{equation}


\item This approach assumes that every grade is ``equally likely,'' which may not be the case.
\item What if there are infinitely many possibilities?
\end{itemize}


\item \textbf{Axiomatic theory}

\begin{itemize}
\item Developed by Kolmogorov in the early 1900s.
\item Identify a few basic axioms that fit intuition.
\item Build a mathematically consistent system based on the axioms.
\item Use measure theory to construct the mathematical system.
\item This is the approach that we will follow.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
