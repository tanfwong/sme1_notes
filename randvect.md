# Random Vector
- First, we will treat $\mathbb{R}^k$ as a vector space and use the
  (column) vector notation to denote its elements. While this is not
  necessary for the development of the underlying probability theory,
  it is however much more convenient.
- Let $\mathcal{B}^k$ be the Borel $\sigma$-field in $\mathbb{R}^k$,
  i.e., $\mathcal{B}^k$ is the $\sigma$-field generated by all the
  half-spaces of the form $(-\infty, x_1) \times(-\infty, x_2) \times
  \cdots \times (-\infty, x_k)$ for all $[x_1,x_2, \ldots, x_k]^T
  \in \mathbb{R}^k$. 
- Starting from the probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$, the
  mapping $\mathbf{X} = [X_1,X_2, \ldots, X_k]^T: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^k$ is called a
  ***random vector*** if $\mathbf{X}^{-1}(S) \in
  \mathcal{F}$ for every $S \in \mathcal{B}^k$, i.e., the mapping is
  measurable.
- Let $P_{\mathbf{X}}$ be the probability measure on $\mathcal{B}^k$ defined by
  $$
  P_{\mathbf{X}}(S) = P\left( \mathbf{X}^{-1}(S) \right) = P\left( \mathbf{X} \in S \right)
  $$
  for every $S \in \mathcal{B}^k$. Then the random vector $\mathbf{X}$
  induces a probability space $\left(\mathbb{R}^k, \mathcal{B}^k,
  P_{\mathbf{X}} \right)$.

- $P_{\mathbf{X}}$ is called the ***joint distribution*** of the
  random vector $\mathbf{X}$. Sometimes, we may use the notation
  $P_{X_1,X_2, \ldots,X_k}$ for the joint distribution to explicitly
  highlight the elements of the random vector $\mathbf{X}$.

- Note that this is a direct generalization of a random pair from the
  special case of $2$ dimensions to the general cased of $k$
  dimensions. Thus, it is conceivable that all the properties,
  concepts, and results about random pairs, their functions, expected
  values, conditional expected values all generalize to random vectors.

- To avoid repetition, we will state here some common generalizations
  that may not be completely unambiguous.
